# Supermarket Sales Analysis & Business Intelligence

## Project Overview

An end-to-end supermarket sales analytics project using Microsoft Excel and Power BI.

The project covers data cleaning, preparation, exploratory analysis, KPI development, customer and product analysis, geographic analysis, and interactive executive dashboards.

## Tools

- Microsoft Excel
- Power Query
- Power BI
- DAX
- PivotTables

## Key Results

- **Total Sales:** $2.26M
- **Unique Customers:** 793
- **Unique Orders:** 4,922
- **Average Revenue per Order:** $459.48
- **Highest Sales Year:** 2018 — $722,052.02
- **Highest Sales Month:** November — $350,161.71
- **Top Category:** Technology — $827,455.87
- **Top Sub-Category:** Phones — $327,782.45
- **Top Region:** West — $710,219.68
- **Highest-Value Customer:** Sean Miller — $25,043.05

## Key Discoveries

### Sales Growth Over Time

Sales increased from **$479,856.21 in 2015** to **$722,052.02 in 2018**. The strongest annual sales performance occurred in 2018, while 2017 also showed a substantial increase compared with 2016.

### Seasonal Sales Pattern

**November generated the highest monthly sales at $350,161.71**, while **February recorded the lowest at $59,371.12**. This indicates a pronounced difference in sales performance across months and provides a useful basis for seasonal planning.

### Category Performance

**Technology generated the highest category sales at $827,455.87**, followed by Furniture and Office Supplies. Within sub-categories, **Phones led with $327,782.45**, while Fasteners generated the lowest sales at $3,001.96.

### Geographic Performance

The **West region generated the highest regional sales at $710,219.68**, followed by East, Central and South. The geographic dashboard provides state- and city-level visibility to help identify where revenue and customer activity are concentrated.

### Customer Concentration

The analysis identified a group of high-value customers contributing substantial revenue, with **Sean Miller generating $25,043.05** in sales. The dashboard's customer concentration view helps stakeholders examine dependence on high-value customers rather than looking only at total customer counts.

### Shipping Patterns

**Standard Class was the most frequently used shipping mode**, with 5,859 transactions, followed by Second Class, First Class and Same Day. This provides an operational view of how customers are being served through different shipping options.

## Business Advice & Recommendations

The findings suggest several areas for management attention:

1. **Use seasonal planning around high-performing months.** November's strong sales performance should be examined when planning inventory, marketing activity and operational capacity for future peak periods.

2. **Protect and develop high-performing categories.** Technology and Phones are major contributors to revenue. Management can investigate the products, customers, locations and periods behind this performance and use those insights when planning future sales activity.

3. **Investigate regional differences.** The variation between regions suggests that sales strategies may need to be examined at regional, state and city levels rather than applying one approach across the entire market.

4. **Strengthen high-value customer relationships.** High-value customers represent an important revenue segment. Customer retention, repeat-purchase analysis and targeted engagement can be considered, subject to additional customer behaviour data.

5. **Review low-performing products and sub-categories.** Low-sales areas such as Fasteners should be investigated further to determine whether the issue relates to demand, product mix, availability, pricing or other factors. The current dataset does not contain enough information to establish the cause.

6. **Use shipping data for operational planning.** Standard Class accounts for the largest share of shipping activity. Further analysis of shipping time, customer satisfaction and regional patterns would help determine whether the current shipping mix is meeting operational expectations.

7. **Build the next layer of analysis with additional data.** The current dataset contains Sales but does not provide Profit, Cost, Discount, Returns, Quantity or Salesperson fields. Adding these fields would allow deeper analysis of profitability, margins, discount impact, return rates, product volume and salesperson performance.

## Important Analytical Limitation

The recommendations above are based on observed sales, customer, product, geographic and shipping patterns. The dataset does **not** contain enough information to establish causal reasons for those patterns.

In particular, profitability and margin analysis cannot be performed reliably because **Profit and Cost are not available**. Likewise, discount effectiveness, returns analysis, quantity-based product performance and salesperson performance require additional fields.

Therefore, the project distinguishes between **what the data shows** and **what should be investigated next**.
